Audioscript
Narrator: One – You hear two students talking about studying abroad? What
did the boy miss?
[male and female speakers, around 14]
Boy: It's good to be home, isn't it?
Girl: Yeah, it's great to sleep in my own bed and catch up with my family. I
hardly spoke to them while I was in Sweden.
Boy: Really? I spoke to mine online all the time. I just had to tell them about my life in Italy. I'll tell you what I missed.
Girl: Let me guess. Your dad's burgers.
Boy: Of course! I had a few in Rome but they were no way near my dad's. The
pasta was amazing though.
Girl: I got a lot better at skating. Did you do any sport?
Boy: I played a bit of football but you know I don't do much sport here either.

Narrator: Two – You hear a young woman talk about her hobby of mountain
biking. What does she say?
(female speaker, in her 20s)
Woman: I love getting out in the mountains and riding my bike. It takes a lot of
physical strength and I think it helps that I've been doing sports since I was at
school. I try to go for a ride every weekend if I can. If I don't, I get grumpy
during the week and more stressed than usual. I usually go with friends and we
pick a different trail every time. One of them is almost a professional—he's
won a couple of local tournaments and he's thinking of competing in a national championship. That's not my thing.

Narrator: Three – You overhear a man talking about his new mobile phone.
What is he complaining about?
[male speaker in his 50s/60s]
Man: Well, I bought it last week; I mean my grandson got it for me over the
internet. It was a lot cheaper than the phones we looked at in that shop on the
High Street. I still can't understand why there's such a difference. It's got lots of
apps and other features I don't know how to use yet. My grandson's teaching
me very patiently. I haven't got used to how big it is yet. My old one was slow
but it was small and handy. It fit in my pocket whereas this one just seems
massive to me.

Narrator: Four – You overhear a young man talking to his mum about his new
job. What does he say is the best thing about it?
[male speaker in his 20s, female speaker in her 50s - telephone conversation]
Woman: So how was your first week at your new job?
Man: Great, Mum! Everybody's really nice to me. My colleagues took me out
for lunch to an Indian restaurant today, so we can get to know each other
better.
Woman: That's nice.
Man: And you know what? My manager told me that I can work from home
once or twice a week if I want to.
Woman: How convenient.
Man: (sounding lower key) Yeah, but I don't think I'll do that. I mean, it's not like I have kids to look after or anything. And the office is so close by I can ride my bike there.
Woman: That was one of the reasons you went for that job, wasn't it?
Man: Yes, that's right. I hated getting the train for my last job.

Narrator: Five – You overhear two friends talking about fear. What is the girl
afraid of now?
(male and female teenage speakers)
Girl: I can't believe you're afraid of thunder and lightning. I mean, if you're
inside your house, you're safe.
Boy: I know, but I've always been nervous when there was a storm. I guess it's
a phobia I have.
Girl: Well, if it makes you feel better, I used to be afraid of ants and all kinds of
bugs when I was little. They're so tiny so I've no idea what scared me about
them. What could they do to me?
Boy: Not much, I suppose. Is there nothing that scares you now?
Girl: I don't like sleeping when it's completely black. I need to be able to see
even if my eyes are shut! (laughs) I usually have a small light in my room.
Boy: I think a lot of people do that.

Narrator: Six – You hear man leave a voice message to his daughter. What
does he ask her to do?
(male speaker in his 40s)
Man: Hi, Emily. Dad here. Remember I told you to pick up the groceries? No
need for that. I was driving past the shop so I got them myself. I forgot to buy
oranges but you don't have to go just for that. Mum would like to watch the new episode of her series tonight. Could you please download it? Use my laptop if you want. I think it's in my office next to those books that are on the floor. I left it charging last night and I can't remember if I unplugged it.

Narrator: Seven – You overhear someone talk about a sailing trip. What
disappointed her about it?
[female speaker, around 16]
Girl: I've been taking sailing lessons for about two years now, so I was really
excited when I went on my first 'live aboard' trip last month. It lasted three days and it was quite intense. It was quite windy and a bit cool, but that's perfect for sailing. The other team members were older and more experienced than me. I felt slow and frustrated. But they all encouraged me to keep on sailing. We sailed along the coast and even caught a glimpse of some dolphins and seals. I'd love to do it again soon.

Narrator: Eight – You hear two classmates talking about their new history
teacher. What do they agree about?
[male and female speakers, around 13]
Girl: What do you think of our new history teacher? He's a lot more fun than Mr Lewis, isn't he?
Boy: Do you think so? I thought Mr Lewis was funny and he gave us quizzes
every week. I found today's lesson really boring.
Girl: Well, give him a chance! I think he's nice and not strict at all.
Boy: You're right about that. Mark and John were chatting all the time and he
didn't get cross.
Girl: No! I know. Did you manage to take any notes? I made some but there
were lots of bits I missed.
Boy: Me too. We should ask him to speak more slowly.
Girl: Yeah, good idea.

3c Read Zetao's letter of application for the position of English assistant. Underline the parts in his letter that refer to the three points in the task in 3b. 
⟦TABLE⟧
Dear Mrs Lee,
I am writing to apply for the position of assistant at the English Club that I saw advertised last week. I am 14 years old and I believe I am an ideal candidate for this role.

I am very interested in working at the after-school club because I love speaking English and this is a great opportunity for me to practise. I also have a lot of free time and I would love to meet new people.

I believe I would be a great assistant because I always get top marks in English lessons. My teacher at school says I am very good at writing. Moreover, I have spent some time in the USA on holidays so I have a very good accent. My friends say that I am very kind and polite, so I think I would get along with the other students at the club.

As for previous experience, I have a younger sister and I usually help her with schoolwork. It is often hard work, but I am patient and I enjoy helping her.

I look forward to receiving your reply as soon as possible.

Yours sincerely,
Chen Zetao

• why you would like to apply: I love speaking English; opportunity for me to practise; a lot of free time; meet new people
• what skills you have that make you suitable for the job: top marks in English; very good at writing; a very good accent; kind and polite
• what previous experience you have of helping other students: a younger sister; help her with schoolwork
