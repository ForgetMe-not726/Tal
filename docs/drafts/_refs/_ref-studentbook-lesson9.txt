文章图片如下：




IQ stands for Intelligence Quotient and it's a score people get on a specially designed test. These tests try to find out how intelligent a person is. An average score on an IQ test is between 90 and 110. Some well­-known people who are believed to have very high IQ scores include the scientist Stephen Hawking, the writer Marilyn vos Savant and the mathematician Terence Tao. 
However, not everyone believes that tests, like IQ tests or school exams, are a good way to measure intelligence. They argue that there are lots of different kinds of intelligence, and that a single test only offers information about one kind. For example, Albert Einstein failed the general part of a college entrance exam at 16, but his exceptional talent in maths and physics still led him to become a brilliant and famous scientist.

From a Small Town to Oxford: Nadia's Story
Last year, Nadia was in her final year of secondary school. Maths and science were compulsory subjects, but she also loved other intellectual activities, like reading and solving problems. Her teachers said she was a bright student because she always asked good questions.
Nadia dreamed of studying Philosophy, Politics, and Economics at Oxford University. Getting admission to this course is very difficult. Her family did not have much money, so they could not educate her abroad. Her only chance was to win a scholarship.
To prepare, Nadia asked Mr Ahmed, a retired teacher, to be her tutor. He helped her for free because he believed in her. Every week, he gave her an assignment—an essay or a maths problem. Nadia also started a small project to help older people in her town learn to read.
Her hard work paid off. Now, Nadia is a student at Oxford. Every day, she listens to a lecturer talk about political theories, and she loves it. She hopes to graduate with honours—and one day return to help her hometown.

3a You are going to read an article in which three teenagers discuss what they are going to do after secondary school. Read the introduction to the article and discuss in pairs. What do you think it'll be like to leave secondary school and go to college or university? 

What next?
People sometimes say that the day you leave secondary school is when 'real life' begins. Some teenagers worry about what they're going to do when school is over. Others are excited about the change. How do you feel about this? 

3b Read quickly what the teenagers say. Which student knows exactly what career they plan to purse?

A James

I can't believe I'm leaving school next month! It only feels like yesterday when I was in Year 6 of primary school. I'm quite positive I'll get good marks in all subjects including those that are a real pain. You see, this year my parents paid a private tutor, Chris, to help me out with maths and physics. He explains stuff a lot more clearly than my school teacher and he tells me I'm very bright. I now feel much more self-confident thanks to him. I haven't made up my mind about what I'm going to do at university, but what I do know is I'm going to have a big party after my exams. They finish on 10 July. It's going to be great!

B Lisa

Although my last day of school is still a long way away, I can't stop worrying about the future. My mother is a university lecturer in geology and my father works in a lab. So, naturally, they want me to do a science degree and then continue to study science after I graduate, but I don't think it's for me. I've decided I'm going to become a music teacher or maybe work for a charity. I've been volunteering at an animal shelter this year and I've never felt so useful in my life. I'm just not interested in doing anything too scientific or intellectual—I prefer doing things that are creative or that make me feel useful by helping others. Hopefully, my parents will understand.

C Tomas

My dream is to become a doctor and travel around the world helping people in troubled areas. My teachers say I'm a really motivated learner and I should definitely apply for admission to a medical school. The problem is that the tuition fees in the USA are incredibly high! My parents can't afford to pay them, so I'm already saving up as much as I can. I do weekend jobs, I'm going to work throughout the summer and I'll have to work while I'm at university too. Even so, I'll probably have to get a student loan to cover everything. What reassures me is that, as a doctor, I'll earn a good enough salary to pay it off. I can't wait for university!
